How to increase your typing speed: a practical plan

Fast typing is a habit, not a talent. Here is a plan that works for English and Hindi.

1. Put accuracy before speed

Beginners often push for speed and end up making many mistakes. Every mistake costs time to notice and correct, and in exam scoring it also costs marks. Type at a pace where you make very few errors, and let speed rise on its own as your fingers learn the keys.

2. Learn the home row

Rest your left fingers on A, S, D, F and your right fingers on J, K, L and the semicolon key. Your thumbs rest on the space bar. After every key, return your fingers to these positions. With the Remington Gail layout the physical keys are the same, so the habit carries over to Hindi.

3. Practise a little every day

Ten to fifteen focused minutes a day beats one long session a week. Short, regular practice gives your brain time to store each movement.

4. Use real passages

Exams use full sentences with punctuation, not random letters. Practise with the kind of text you will meet: news, official letters, general essays. Real text also builds the vocabulary that lets you read ahead of your fingers.

5. Review your mistakes

After each test, look at the words you missed. Retype those words five or ten times. Most people repeat the same few errors, so fixing them gives a quick gain.

A simple four-week plan

WeekFocusDaily practice
1Home row, posture, no looking at keys10 minutes, easy text
2All rows, accuracy above 95%15 minutes, practice mode
3Speed with steady rhythm15 minutes, timed tests
4Exam conditionsOne full-length test in exam mode, plus review

6. Track your progress

Look at your average over a week rather than a single test. A bad day is normal. If your average speed and accuracy are both rising, the plan is working.

7. Look after your body

Sit with your back supported, feet flat and wrists relaxed. Keep the screen at eye level and take a short break every 20 to 30 minutes.
