Typing test scoring explained

Full mistakes, half mistakes and net speed, with a worked example.

Why words, not letters?

Many government typing tests compare the words you typed with the original passage. This is different from simple speed tests that count correct letters, so your score on Typing Studio is also calculated by words.

Full mistakes

A full mistake is a word that is left out, a word that is replaced by a different word, or a word that was added and is not in the passage.

Half mistakes

A half mistake is a smaller slip. Examples are a spelling error inside a word, a wrong capital letter, or a spacing error such as two words joined together.

The formula

Total mistakes = full mistakes + half mistakes ÷ 2
Net speed (WPM) = (keystrokes ÷ 5 − total mistakes) ÷ minutes

A “word” in speed calculation is five keystrokes, so keystrokes divided by five gives the number of standard words you typed.

A worked example

You type for 15 minutes and make 3,000 keystrokes. That is 3,000 ÷ 5 = 600 standard words. You made 20 full mistakes and 10 half mistakes, so total mistakes = 20 + 10 ÷ 2 = 25. Net speed = (600 − 25) ÷ 15 = about 38 words per minute.

Ignorable mistakes

Some exams ignore a small share of mistakes, for example 5% of the words typed, before they apply the penalty. Others do not. The rules differ between exams, so check your own notification before you rely on any calculator, including this one.

How to lose fewer marks

  • Do not skip a word you cannot read. Type your best guess instead.
  • Watch spacing: one space between words, no extra spaces.
  • Do not retype a word you already typed.
  • Practise in exam mode so you get used to having no backspace.
