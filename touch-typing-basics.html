Touch typing basics

Touch typing means typing without looking at the keys. It is the foundation of fast, accurate typing.

Finger placement

Place your left fingers on A, S, D, F and your right fingers on J, K, L and the semicolon. These are the home row keys. Many keyboards have small bumps on F and J so you can find them by touch. Each finger is responsible for the keys above and below its home key. Your thumbs handle the space bar.

Posture

  • Sit upright with your back supported and your feet flat on the floor.
  • Keep your elbows close to your body, bent at roughly a right angle.
  • Keep your wrists straight and relaxed, hovering over the keys.
  • Place the screen at eye level, about an arm’s length away.

Rhythm

Aim for an even beat, with the same gap between every key. Uneven typing, fast bursts followed by pauses, usually leads to mistakes. If you slow down a little and keep the beat steady, your speed will rise naturally.

Do not look down

It is tempting to check the keys, but it slows your learning. Use the on-screen keyboard to see which key comes next, and try to feel the key with your finger instead of looking at your hands.

Common beginner problems

  • Using only two or three fingers: it feels quick at first but limits your speed later.
  • Pressing too hard: light touches are faster and less tiring.
  • Not returning to the home row: always bring your fingers back after each key.

Take breaks

Stop and stretch every 20 to 30 minutes. If your hands or wrists hurt, stop and rest, and check your posture and desk height.
